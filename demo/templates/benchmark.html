What the numbers mean

Required items leaked
Of all the items the policy says must not survive (names, addresses, dates of birth, phone numbers, ID numbers, signatures…), the share the tool did not remove or replace: visible on the page, partly visible, faintly visible under a see-through box, still in the file's hidden text or earlier versions; or not handled at all, because the tool produced no output or, for a tool graded on its text, because the item was never in the text the tool's own reader took from the PDF (running headers and footers, for example). Items of that last kind are not in the tool's output, so nothing there can be read from it, but nothing shows the tool would have removed them either. For a tool graded on its text, the headline is the leak rate on the items it did handle; the row also says how many items never reached its output and gives the strict rate, which counts them against the tool. For a tool graded on its PDF, the headline is the strict rate, and a second number under it, when shown, leaves out the items it never handled. A tool graded on its PDF is graded on those same headers and footers in the PDF, so read both numbers before comparing. Lower is better; 0% means nothing required got through. The 95% range shows how much the number could move with other documents of the same kind.
Not replaced
Required items that were hidden but not swapped for a realistic fake, which the client's rules ask for. Not a leak, but not a pass either.
Over-redaction
The share of ordinary words, which should have stayed, that the tool removed or damaged. Lower is better. A word that was only moved along its line, intact, does not count.
Clean documents
Documents with nothing wrong: no required item visible, partly visible or recoverable, every required item checked and replaced as required, nothing found in the metadata, no protected word damaged, at most 1% of ordinary words damaged and, for a PDF, at least 95% of the kept words still searchable. These could be released as they are.
Graded on: PDF or text only
A PDF is checked as a reader would get it: every page image, the hidden text layer, metadata and earlier versions. A tool that outputs only text is graded on its text instead: its redacted text is compared with the text it started from. That cannot check signatures, images, handwriting, hidden layers or searchability, so a text-graded tool is judged on less than a PDF-graded one, and its numbers are not a like-for-like comparison on those points.
Searchable
For a PDF, the share of kept words that can still be found by search or copied at their place on the page (the client asked for searchable output). Not checked for text-only output.
Plumbing check
A run made to test the connection to a tool, not the tool itself (a fake model in place of the real one, or a setting changed to run on a laptop). Its numbers are not the tool's scores.
What each tool is graded on
The output the tool produces on its own for an upload. For the PrivateGPT tool that is its redacted text right after the upload. Its optional second pass is not run, here or in the benchmark: in its workspace, "Check Now" (which also runs when a project is opened) suggests fixes for items it may have missed, and "Fix All" applies them on a click.
Tool's own release check
Some tools decide themselves whether a document is safe to release. The benchmark compares that decision with its own verdict: released and clean, held back and not clean (both right), held back although clean (a false alarm), or released although not clean (an unsafe release).

Full definitions: docs/metrics.md in the benchmark.
